What is Nightwatch?

Nightwatch combines Devin Automations with TypeSafe’s Jev to screen transfers and recommend an action to the bank’s back office. The bank decides what to do with the transfer.

  1. 1

    Algorithmic rules

    A transfer qualifies if it meets the amount and 24-hour limits and shows no takeover red flags.

    Eligible → Recommend allow

  2. →
    2

    Preflight check

    One Jev call evaluates several plain-language questions and returns a structured answer for each.

    Clear result → Recommend action

  3. →
    3

    Devin review

    Review cases when Jev reports low confidence, returns conflicting answers, or fails. Give the back office reasons for the recommendation.

    Findings → Back office

Example check · Does this sign-in show signs of account takeover given the account’s history?

Speaker notes 0:35–1:00

0:35 · Trace the three routes. Move left to right. Give Jev’s two answers and Devin’s escalation one beat each.

“Rules can recommend release without a model call. Otherwise, Devin Preflight asks Jev two questions in one call. How likely is takeover? What kind of sign-in is this? Low confidence, conflicting answers or a failed check trigger a Devin review. Let’s follow that route.”

Routing detail for rehearsal

The rules check the amount, recent transfer totals and takeover flags. Jev returns a takeover probability and a sign-in classification with its own confidence. Preflight escalates when classification confidence is below 50%, when the probability and classification conflict under the routing rules, or when the check fails. The bank owns the final action.

Why Nightwatch?

  • 298 ms

    Median time to a valid Jev response.

  • 60% lower

    Estimated model cost per call vs. GPT‑6 Luna with caching disabled.

  • Less data to share

    One API takes agreed risk signals. Names, account IDs and raw IPs stay with the bank.

  • Quick to add checks

    Risk teams write questions in plain language. Engineers test and deploy them without retraining the model.

  • Improve with feedback

    Roadmap · The bank confirms outcomes, Devin proposes revisions, and the team tests and rolls out approved checks.

Prototype benchmark · 18 synthetic sign-ins · One question per call

“For a pilot, we’d agree which risk signals to send and keep names, account IDs and raw IP addresses inside the bank. We’d also keep the decision trail in the bank’s own systems. In our first-pass test with caching disabled, Jev took about 300 milliseconds at the median and had a 60 percent lower estimated model cost per call than GPT‑6 Luna.”

Benchmark detail for rehearsal

Saved run compare-t0qr66tt compares Jev 1.14.0 with gpt-6-luna via /v1/chat/completions, using Structured Outputs and the standard tier. Both returned valid answers on all 18 cases and met the predefined probability ranges on the six cases with range checks. Luna recorded zero cache reads and writes.

Cost estimates use observed tokens and public list rates. Jev used 33,801 input tokens. Luna used 24,611 input and 2,205 completion tokens, including reasoning. In the cached replay compare-80hti35t, Luna recorded 23,593 cached input tokens and projected to $76.29 per million calls. Jev projected to $78.87 in both runs.

Product roadmap

More context. Better questions.

Phase 1 · Prototype

Sign-in risk

Rules check the transfer. Jev assesses the sign-in.

Phase 2 · Next

Activity and transfers

Use recent account actions and transfer patterns, such as new payees or a burst of payments.

Phase 3 · Later

Improve Jev’s questions

A scheduled Devin Automation proposes changes based on outcomes confirmed by the bank.

Phase 3 · Feedback loop

  1. Confirm outcomesThe bank verifies cases reported by customers or support
  2. Propose revisionsDevin checks Jev’s answers against confirmed outcomes
  3. Replay both versionsThe same held-out test cases
  4. Compare resultsFraud caught, false holds and score calibration
